## Scoring rubric (priority 1–5)
- **+** strong commercial/local intent (someone ready to hire)
- **+** realistic to rank (geo-modified, not nationally saturated)
- **+** ties to a high-margin service (water heaters, sump pumps, repipe, sewer)
- **+** matches a Calgary-specific pain (hard water, freeze, basement flood)
- **−** purely informational with weak conversion path
- **−** too broad / unwinnable for one city
- 5 = high intent + winnable + core service. 1 = informational, low conversion.

## What works (learnings)
- **Localize everything.** "emergency plumber Calgary" >> "emergency plumber". National cost figures should be reframed with Calgary pricing.
- **Calgary hard water** is a recurring, differentiated angle: water heater flushes, water softeners, fixture/heater lifespan. Reuse across topics.
- **Seasonal cornerstone content** (frozen pipes, sump pump, winterization) is evergreen + spikes Nov–Mar. Build/refresh before Q4.
- **Neighbourhood/community pages** are a proven pattern for FlameTech (they already have Auburn Bay, Cranston, Chaparral, Aspen Woods etc.). Expanding these is low-risk SEO.
- **Cross-sell HVAC**: FlameTech does heating + AC; "no heat emergency" and "AC repair Calgary" are owned territory most plumbing lists ignore.

3. **⚑ DROP what we already have + CONCENTRATE ON BLOG POSTS (owner-directed Run 16).** The owner wants the list to surface *new* work, not re-catalogue the existing site.
   - **Prune anything already published.** Entries tagged **✅ (a dedicated page exists)** are NOT active targets — pull them out of the active SP tables and Top Priorities. Keep a single condensed **"Already live — reference only, do not re-list"** block so a run knows the page exists (for internal-linking / cluster pillars) without it cluttering the target list. ⚠️ (content exists, no dedicated URL) and ❌ (absent) STAY — those are still real work (a carve-out or a new build). A ✅ only earns a spot if there's a specific *refresh* action, and even then it lives in the reference block, not the priority list.
   - **Blog posts are the priority lane now.** Each run's focused improvement should default to the BLOG POSTS section — add new BP ideas, deepen clusters with spokes, mine question/PAA long-tails, refine titles — unless a ⚠️/❌ service-page gap is clearly higher-value that run. When choosing the single improvement, weight BP over SP. The blog is both the owner's stated focus and our fastest 🟢 lane, so this reinforces existing principle #3.
   - This does **not** mean delete history or stop tracking that a page exists — it means the *forward-looking target list* should be dominated by blog posts and by SP gaps (⚠️/❌), never by ✅ pages we've already built.

## Ranking-realism rubric (🟢/🟡/🔴)
- 🟢 **Realistic (0–6 mo):** page exists/minor work, OR informational/blog SERP, OR long-tail. Reviews + relevance suffice.
- 🟡 **Moderate (6–12 mo):** competitive SERP, reachable with a dedicated page + content/links + local-pack push.
- 🔴 **Hard (12 mo+; #1 organic unlikely):** national franchises (Mr. Rooter) + exact-match domains + 10+ yr high-DA incumbents on head terms. Pursue **local pack (GBP) + long-tail variants** instead.
- Pattern observed: **head service terms → 🟡/🔴; informational/blog + long-tail → 🟢.** Blog + long-tail is where FlameTech moves the needle fastest.

## Durable principles (consolidated — challenge & merge these, don't append new dated layers)
*This is the living playbook. When a run produces a lesson, fold it INTO the relevant principle below (sharpen, merge, or overturn it) instead of adding a "Run N learnings" section. Run numbers appear only as provenance for a specific correction. History lives in changelog.md / thinking-log.md — not here.*

**1. Localize and ground everything.** Geo-modified terms win; bare national terms don't. Bank every real Calgary $ figure a search surfaces into the list/briefs and reuse it — a cost/trust block is the spine of any converting page, so credible local numbers are non-negotiable. Embed a real range in suggested titles. When no clean local figure is published, triangulate a defensible range + an explicit "verify" note; never invent false precision.

**2. Score realism from the live SERP, never from a heuristic.** "Differentiated niche," "we have a page," and "minor gap" have ALL produced wrong 🟢s (Poly-B, water softener, faucet — Runs 9 & 13). Audit the *easy-looking* 🟢s hardest; they escape scrutiny and go stale. Use the Competitor Profile Sheet to keep scoring consistent: classify competitors by threat *type* (franchise heavyweight = 🔴 head terms; full-scope locals = 🟡; niche specialist/EMD = 🔴 *in its niche only*; mid-tier = our beatable field). A niche specialist locks one cluster, not the vertical — score per-SERP.

**3. Win where incumbents structurally can't.** Incumbents under-invest in blogs → informational/blog SERPs are FlameTech's fastest 🟢 lane. When a SERP is owned by a *different business type* (restoration firms on "flood cleanup"; **insurance sites on "does my policy cover a flooded basement" — Run 17**), don't fight their term — answer the same query from the service you uniquely sell (prevention: backwater valve + sump + battery backup) and route traffic to your pillar. **The bridge is strongest when the other vertical's product *requires* yours: Run 18 verified that many Alberta insurers now *require a backwater valve to grant sewer-backup coverage* (and discount for backwater valve + sump) — so the coverage rule itself makes the install the reader's next step.** Hunt for these "you must buy X to get Y" dependencies; they convert better than a generic risk-reduction pitch. These "owned by another vertical" queries are often the highest-conversion blog hooks precisely because no plumber is answering them. Subsidy/rebate/**code-mandate** hooks are the highest-conversion local angle: build the asset around the *complete claim/compliance how-to*, not the announcement that one exists, which makes you the obvious provider. Two hook flavours, ranked: a **code MANDATE with a fine + an annual recurrence** (backflow testing — Bylaw 40M2006 §39.3e, $1,500 fine, every spring) beats a one-time *subsidy* (backwater valve) because it adds repeat revenue to the conversion pull. Rebate programs change fast and asymmetrically — the *closure* of a big grant (Greener Homes, Oct 2025) creates as much fresh search demand ("what's still available?") as a launch; treat program churn as an opportunity, verify amounts annually, and kill dead programs in the list (Calgary toilet rebate ended 2016) so they're not re-chased.

**4. Judge each entry on two axes: winnable AND worth it.** Rankable ≠ worth a dedicated page. Low-margin terms (faucet install $125–$200) get bundled, not built. Some commercial terms (sewer camera, often free) are lead-gen *loss-leaders* → tag their funnel role (satellite of a money pillar), not just win-prob.

**5. Each run, fix the actual bottleneck — coverage, usability, or execution.** This is the core recurring question. Coverage (more keywords) was the bottleneck early; then usability (calendar, build queue, competitor sheet, cluster map — reusable references that make existing work *actionable*); then execution (writer-ready briefs, one cluster per run, depth-first). Restraint/subtraction is a real move — a long backlog of un-actioned "next-run" flags is itself the signal that cleanup is overdue. Don't let the easy add crowd out the disciplined prune. **A new discovery isn't "done" until it's wired into the executable plan (queue + cluster), self-consistently.** A re-score that touches one view (the SP table) but not the parallel view (the Page-Build Queue) leaves the document contradicting itself — and the *stale* view is the one a builder might act on. Run 15 found backflow scored Pri 4/dedicated-SP in the list but still #11/"don't build a page" in the queue. Closing discovery→queue→cluster drift is often higher-value than any new add: pipeline is discovery → re-score → queue slot → cluster wiring → brief, and a run's job is to advance ONE item one stage *completely*, not half-update it across views.

**6. Hygiene rules.** One canonical row per keyword (cross-reference, never duplicate) — and keep the **parallel views in sync** (SP table ↔ Page-Build Queue ↔ Cluster Map); when one is re-scored, propagate to the others in the same run or they drift (Run 15). New deliverable *types* get their own file (briefs → `content-briefs.md`), wired to the list with a pointer. Carve-outs (⚠️, content exists) beat from-scratch (❌) in the queue — effort is a real input. **Season-scheduling is a first-class queue concept, not an exception:** high-value builds are slotted by their pre-spike draft window, not pure value rank (frozen-pipes = fall build for Jan; backflow = spring build for the Apr–May bylaw deadline). Publish seasonal content ~4–6 weeks BEFORE the spike; use the calendar as the tie-breaker between equally-good items. Close standing "verify" flags opportunistically; flag subsidy figures verify-*annually* (a stale subsidy misdirects a government application — worse than a stale price).
